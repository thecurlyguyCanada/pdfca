## How Do I Scan a Document to PDF With My Phone for Free?

Six steps. Most of the time goes into taking the photos; creating a 10-page PDF took about 2 seconds in our tests.

### Step 1: Frame the page before you shoot

This is the step people get wrong, because most scanning apps do it for them. This tool has no automatic edge detection, cropping or straightening: whatever is in the photo ends up on the page. So:

- lay the paper flat on a plain surface, preferably darker than the paper;
- hold the phone directly above the page, parallel to it, not at an angle;
- fill the frame with the page, leaving only a thin border;
- use even light and keep your own shadow off the paper.

The tool reminds you of this under the buttons: "There is no automatic cropping or straightening."

### Step 2: Tap Take photo, or Choose photos

**Take photo** asks your phone's browser to open the rear camera directly. Take the picture, confirm it, and it appears as Page 1. The button then reads **Take another photo**; tap it once per page.

**Choose photos** opens your photo library or files instead, so you can pick pictures you took earlier, several at a time. On a computer, both buttons open the file picker, and you can also drop images onto the page.

### Step 3: Check the order and rotation

Each photo shows as a thumbnail labelled Page 1, Page 2 and so on, with four buttons: **Rotate 90°** (clockwise), **Move up**, **Move down** and **Delete**. Photos taken sideways are normally turned upright automatically from the orientation the camera recorded; use **Rotate 90°** for any that are not. Delete the blurry shot and retake it.

### Step 4: Pick an enhancement

Under **Enhancement**, choose **Original colour** (the default), **Grayscale** or **Black & white document**. The choice applies to all pages, and the thumbnails update to show the result, so you can judge before you create the file. For a typed letter, a T4 or a receipt, **Black & white document** is usually the clearest and smallest; the next section compares the three.

### Step 5: Pick a page size

Under **Page size**, choose **Letter** (the default, 8.5 × 11 in), **A4**, or **Fit to photo**. Letter and A4 place the photo centred inside a ¼-inch margin and switch to landscape for a landscape photo. **Fit to photo** makes each page the shape of the photo itself, with no margin, which suits receipts and ID cards.

### Step 6: Tap Create PDF and download

The status line shows "Processing page 3 of 10…". When it finishes, the result card shows the file name, its size and the page count. Tap **Download**. The file is named with today's date, such as `scan-2026-11-12.pdf`. **Start over** clears everything for the next document.

## Colour, Grayscale or Black & White: Which Should I Choose?

Choose by what is on the paper. These are the sizes we measured for the same 10 phone photos on Letter pages:

| Enhancement | What it does | Best for | 10 photos, Letter |
|---|---|---|---|
| Original colour | Keeps the photo as taken | Coloured forms, photos, stamps, highlighted text | 2.86 MB |
| Grayscale | Removes colour and stretches the contrast | Pencil notes, faint print, when colour adds nothing | 5.31 MB on our test photos |
| Black & white document | Turns paper white and text black, adjusting for local lighting | Typed letters, tax slips, receipts, printed forms | 1.37 MB |

A word on grayscale: it does not reliably make the file smaller. Its contrast stretch also strengthens the grain in a phone photo, and on our noisy test photos that made it larger than colour. Choose it for legibility, not size.

**Black & white document** is the setting that makes a phone photo look like a scan. It compares each pixel with the brightness of the paper around it rather than using one cut-off for the whole page, so a shadow across one corner or a page that is brighter in the middle still comes out as white paper with black text. Smooth edges are kept on the letters. Two things to know: anything that is not paper or ink, such as a dark tabletop at the edges, tends to turn into black bands or speckles, and colours disappear, so a red "Paid" stamp becomes grey or black.

Per page, the sizes varied a lot with the subject: a receipt photo was about 49 KB in colour and 70 KB in black & white, while a full page shot with uneven lighting and a dark table around it was 332 KB in colour and 484 KB in black & white, because of the speckled border.

## How Do I Take a Photo That Scans Well?

Because nothing is cropped or straightened afterwards, the photo is the scan. A few habits make the difference:

1. **Shoot from directly above.** A photo taken at an angle gives a page that is wider at the bottom than at the top, and it stays that way in the PDF.
2. **Fill the frame.** The more of the photo is paper, the bigger and sharper the text on the PDF page.
3. **Use daylight or a bright room, no flash.** A flash leaves a glare spot that can wash out a word. Black & white document handles gentle shadows well; it cannot recover text lost in glare.
4. **Hold still and let the camera focus.** Tap the text on screen to focus if your camera allows it.
5. **Flatten the page.** A fold or a curled receipt bends the lines of text. Weigh the corners down.

## How Do I Scan Tax Slips and Receipts for a CRA Claim?

### Receipts for a medical expense or moving claim

Lay out the receipts one at a time, take a photo of each, and choose **Black & white document** and **Fit to photo**, so each page is the shape of the receipt rather than a narrow strip on a big white page. One PDF per claim keeps the CRA's request for supporting documents simple to answer. If thermal-paper receipts are fading, keep **Original colour** or **Grayscale**, which keep faint print that a black-and-white threshold might drop.

### A T4 or T5 slip someone asked you for

A lender or an accountant wants your T4. Photograph it on a dark surface, choose **Black & white document** and **Letter**, and create the PDF. Check that every box number and amount is readable on the result before you send it. To let them search or copy the numbers, run the file through [OCR PDF](/en/ocr-pdf).

### An IRCC supporting document

Immigration portals accept PDFs and limit the size of each upload, often to around 4 MB. A few pages in **Black & white document** on Letter usually land well under that; if not, run the file through [Compress PDF](/en/compress-pdf). Scan a passport or a coloured certificate in **Original colour**, since stamps, photos and security printing carry meaning there. Check the portal's instructions for what needs to be in colour.

### A signed lease page or form going back to a landlord or employer

Print, sign, photograph, send. Use **Black & white document** for a crisp signature and small file. If the other pages already exist as a PDF, add the signed page to them with [Merge PDF](/en/merge-pdf).

### A two-sided document

Photograph the front, then the back, as consecutive pages. If you shot all the fronts first and then all the backs, use **Move up** and **Move down** to interleave them before creating the PDF.

## Can I Search or Copy the Text in My Scan?

Not until you run OCR. Every page of the PDF is a photograph, so the file has no text in it: Ctrl+F finds nothing and a screen reader reads nothing. The result card links to the fix: open [OCR PDF](/en/ocr-pdf), add the scan, and it adds an invisible text layer in French and English while the page looks exactly the same.

If you need to compress as well, compress first and run OCR on the compressed file, so the strongest compression levels do not discard the text layer.

## What Happens to My Photos?

They stay in your browser. Each photo is read from your camera or files, shrunk to at most 2000 pixels on its long edge, kept in the tab's memory with a small thumbnail, and only encoded into the PDF when you tap **Create PDF**. No request sends them anywhere, and closing the tab clears them.

The PDF also leaves out the photo's camera data. Phone photos usually record the time, the phone model and often the GPS location where they were taken. None of that is copied into the PDF, which is one less thing to think about when you send an identity document or a tax slip.

## What If My iPhone Photos Are HEIC?

Safari on iPhone and Mac can open HEIC, so it works there. Chrome, Edge and Firefox generally cannot, and the tool shows a message naming the file with an **Open HEIC to PDF** button. You have three options:

- use [HEIC to PDF](/en/heic-to-pdf), which converts HEIC photos without relying on the browser;
- open this page in Safari instead;
- set the iPhone camera to Most Compatible (Settings, Camera, Formats) so new photos are saved as JPG.

Photos taken with the **Take photo** button are normally handed to the browser as JPEG, so HEIC mostly comes up with pictures picked from the library.

## When Should You Not Use This Tool?

- **You need a perfectly straight, cropped scan from a casual photo.** There is no edge detection or perspective correction. A flatbed scanner, an office copier, or a scanning app with automatic cropping will do better with angled shots.
- **You need searchable text in one step.** The output is image-only; add [OCR PDF](/en/ocr-pdf) afterwards.
- **You want different settings per page.** The enhancement applies to every page. Make two PDFs, one in colour and one in black & white, and combine them with [Merge PDF](/en/merge-pdf).
- **You are combining existing PDFs.** This tool takes images only. Use Merge PDF for PDFs.
- **You need archival or print quality.** Pages are capped at 2000 pixels and saved as JPEG, which suits screens and uploads but not a high-resolution archive of a photograph or artwork. Use [JPG to PDF](/en/jpg-to-pdf) when you want the images kept closer to their original.
- **The document is a book or a thick binder.** Curved pages near the spine stay curved in the photo.
